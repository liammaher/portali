# Chiara the architect: what makes her work hard, and where we fit

Conclusion of the 2026-10-27 strategy discussion, after the one on Gennaro (`Gennaro strategy
conclusion.md` in this folder). Chiara runs a one- or two-person architecture studio in the Comune di
Napoli: home renovations, shop fit-outs for people like Gennaro, the odd sanatoria, and leftover
Superbonus files.

## Why her life is hard

**1. Before she can design anything, she has to dig up the building's past.** Every job starts with the
*stato legittimo*: what was legally built.
- An accesso agli atti at the Comune's archive. The law gives the Comune 30 days, and silence counts as a
  refusal; in practice it can take months, and files go missing.
- Catasto visure and floor plans, and whether an old condono is still pending. Naples has one of Italy's
  biggest condono backlogs, and nobody publishes the number.
- The 2024 Salva Casa decree makes the stato legittimo central, so demand on the archive goes up.
- This is the biggest time sink, and she signs for it.

**2. Five or more bodies, each with its own portal, forms and interpretation.**
- **SUE:** moved to impresainungiorno on 6 July 2026, after 15 years on SisDoc; everything is in transition.
- **Soprintendenza:** the UNESCO centro storico alone is about 1,021 hectares, plus landscape constraints.
  By law it has 45 days for its opinion on an autorizzazione paesaggistica (90 in other procedures), but a
  request for more documents stops the clock. TAR Campania and the Consiglio di Stato disagree on what a
  late opinion is worth.
- **Genio Civile / SISMICA:** a deposito or autorizzazione sismica, with a structural engineer and a geologist.
- Then the ASL notifica preliminare, Vigili del Fuoco and the SUAP for shops, DOCFA at the catasto, the
  agibilità (SCA), the APE.
- The order matters: one authority's approval comes before the next, and a single request for more
  documents delays the whole chain.

**3. She carries criminal liability.** Her asseverazioni stand in for public checks (the Inarcassa
foundation calls the professional's role "vicarious" to them). After Superbonus: on 21 July 2026 the
Guardia di Finanza seized €14.55m in credits for the Procura di Napoli Nord; AdE checks asseverazioni
against emails, invoices and delivery notes; credits can be contested until 31 Dec 2026.

**4. The rules keep moving and contradict each other.** Salva Casa (2024), the 2025 decree on solar
panels in historic centres, the Naples PRG and building rules; a new national building code is approved as
a delega and at least two years away. Ordini have complained for years that bureaucracy takes 80% of the
work and design 20% (a 2019 figure from the Rome Ordine, not Naples).

**5. Her own obligations:** Inarcassa (minimum contributions, the integrativo on 31 Aug, the dichiarazione
31 Oct → 2 Nov, the conguaglio on 31 Dec), 60 training credits per three years (cycle 2026–28), PEC,
firma digitale, invoices. Annoying, but routine.

## What's solved or will be soon

| Part | Status |
|---|---|
| Design, BIM, rendering | Solved, and AI is improving it fast (not admin) |
| Calculations, APE, Legge 10, cost estimates | Solved by software (ACCA, Blumatica and the like) |
| Filing at the SUE | Solved-ish: impresainungiorno since July 2026, with tracking |
| Her taxes and Inarcassa | Solved: commercialista, AI accountants |
| Drafting reports, reading the rules, client emails, paperwork | Very soon: Claude, Duet, Muse |
| Signing, identity, mandates | Solved or soon: firma digitale, IT-Wallet, the EU Business Wallet |
| A national AI system checking CILA, SCIA and permits ("EdilIA") | Unverified: one trade outlet reports a 2025 decree creating it, not confirmed officially |

## What's unlikely to be solved soon

- **The Naples building archive:** paper, slow, files lost; no plan to digitise it was found.
- **How each Naples office actually decides:** what the Soprintendenza accepts on a façade in Chiaia, what
  the SUE typically asks for, how long the Genio Civile really takes. All of it unwritten.
- **What applies to this address and this intervention:** UNESCO zone, constraint, PRG zone, seismic
  route, pending condono, and in which order. The data exists, scattered across maps and offices.

## The gap, and our fit

The same two holes as Gennaro's, but deeper, and we fit better here.

**1. Naples rules, by address.** "For this intervention at this address, which authorities, in what
order, with which documents?" The library already has the CILA (current with the July 2026 switch), the
dehors, the insegna, the SUAP and fire safety; the vault already works out a street's municipalità, and
the library already uses the UNESCO zones for bars and restaurants. Missing: SCIA edilizia, permesso di
costruire, accesso agli atti, autorizzazione paesaggistica, deposito/autorizzazione sismica, notifica
preliminare, SCA, sanatoria.

**2. Outcomes, from the people who file the most.** "Soprintendenza, Chiaia, façade: they asked for
colour samples and a photo survey; 70 days." Architects are far better contributors than Gennaro: he does
one pratica every few years, she does dozens a year, and they already swap exactly this on WhatsApp
groups and at the Ordine. They're also the distribution channel: every architect files for many Gennaros.

**One hole we should not fill: a shared history of each building.** Tempting, since every architect
rebuilds the same building's past from scratch. But it would put potential abusi on a map and expose
owners to enforcement, carry liability for anything wrong in it, and it's the Comune's job. Collect
outcomes per office and procedure, never per building.

## What this changes in the plan

- **Start with architects (and geometri), not owners.** They file far more pratiche, report more, trust
  sourced rules, and bring the owners along.
- **Next procedures for the library:** the building-works chain above, in the order the authorities require.
- **Open the outcomes network with the Soprintendenza, the SUE and the archive**, where the unwritten
  knowledge is worth the most.
- **Partners:** the Ordine degli Architetti di Napoli (its continuing-education credits are a natural
  reward for contributing) and the Ordine degli Ingegneri.
